# The interface audit

The rules below are binding; `CLAUDE.md` sends you here before you report as done any change the interface shows, and before you change `scripts/audit-ui.mjs` or `scripts/fake-steam.mjs`.

## Rules

Tests passing is not the end of a change that touches the interface. Every new or changed screen, section or control is audited in the running app before it is reported as done, and the report says what was audited and what was not. The steps, every time:

1. **Run `pnpm audit:ui`** (`scripts/audit-ui.mjs`). It runs once per language. `AUDIT_LANGUAGES=fr pnpm audit:ui` runs one language while working on it; the full run is what counts.
2. **Add the new thing to the script** when the audit cannot reach it as it is: a new screen, a section that has to be opened, a state behind a click. A feature the audit never opens has not been audited. Two kinds of thing always get a flow check of their own: **a control that opens something is audited in both directions**, and **a shortcut that leads to an item is audited with every filter that could hide the item turned on**.
3. **Look at it under the pointer.** The checks say that a hover changes something, not what it looks like, and every capture is at rest. `captureHover` in the script saves a capture with the hover forced: use it for anything new that can be clicked, and look at the result. (Written down after a tile lit up with two thirds of it empty and nobody had seen it hovered.)
4. **Prove a new check can fail**: undo the fix it is meant to guard, run the audit, see it fail, restore. A check that was only ever seen passing may be checking nothing.
5. **Look at the captures** it saves to `.audit-ui/`, every one that the change could have touched. The checks pass on whatever is on screen, so a capture of the wrong screen is the only sign that the script went astray; it has happened more than once.
6. **Check by eye what no script measures**, on those captures: lines and separators (one between two things, never two in a row and none at the end of a group), alignment of text with the page margin, spacing between groups, text that wraps or is cut in Spanish and French, and anything said twice on the same screen.
7. **Open by hand what the script does not:** dialogs other than the one that removes an account (the pasted list of a checklist, the update that is ready), the update screen, a card's checklist and note, toasts other than the one that announces a followed account, the pressed state, and real avatars and pictures. **Say in the report what was audited and what was not**, including which of these were not opened.

- After changing a screen, also audit it element by element and in every state (lists open, dialogs open, sections expanded): pointer cursor, accessible name, reachable by keyboard, and a visible change on hover, on press and on keyboard focus. Then run axe.
- To do that reliably, force `:hover`, `:active` and `:focus-visible` through the DevTools protocol and compare computed styles. Park the mouse pointer first, or a really hovered element hides a missing hover style.
- **The audit runs on a developer's machine only.** It is not part of CI nor of the commit and push hooks: it takes several minutes, needs a display and fetches pictures from Steam.
- **The fake Steam is reachable only when the app is not packaged.**

The accessibility rules the audit checks are in `docs/interface.md`.

## What the audit covers

It builds the app and runs it for real against a **fake Steam** (`scripts/fake-steam.mjs`), a local server that answers the Web API's calls from the real responses in `__tests__/fixtures` and lets the audit change what "Steam" says, and against a fake Steam client folder. Nothing of this computer's account, key or data is read, and the result is the same on every run. **The audit runs once per language, as a new user each time**, so everything on screen is in that one language from the first screen on: interface, errors, and the achievement names and descriptions, which the fake Steam answers in the language asked for (the audit fails when a name on screen is not Steam's in that language). In each language it:

- **goes through the onboarding**, picking the language on its first screen, with the SteamID found in the Steam client and locked, and Steam rejecting the key (in the first language with Enter held down in the key field, which must ask Steam once), being unreachable and hiding the profile before it accepts, until the account step lets the app be entered;
- **checks every screen** (Game, the open game details, Dashboard, Settings top and end): accessibility with axe; no scrollbar on the window itself and nothing overflowing sideways; a visible change on hover and on keyboard focus for every kind of clickable element (both states are forced and the computed styles compared); and no clickable element wider than what contains it, which is how a hover wash spills out of its row;
- **makes the interface fail to draw** and checks the crash screen like any other, that the error reached `logs/errors.log`, and that "Reload" brings the app back.

Then, in the first language only, **the flows no single capture shows**: an account Steam accepts when it is checked and rejects as it is saved (the form stays open with what was typed and the reason); "Use another account" opening the SteamID for typing, without saying that no account was found; the only account removed in the setup while "Add another account" is open (the form of the first account comes back, with the SteamID of the Steam client, and the account is verified again); the game details animating open and closed (the height is sampled on every frame) and leading to each achievement they name with the hidden-only filter on; a game starting (the app switches to it by itself, from another tab); an achievement unlocked while playing, which the app has to notice with nobody touching it (it leaves the pending list, the notice names it, the header counts one less); the last one being unlocked (the completion state, and the completion date on the game's row of the dashboard before anything reads the dashboard again); the game closing (it stays on screen as the last one played); two accounts (the second added in the account step of the setup, with the form put away after each; switching by hand; the app following the account that signs in to Steam, with its toast; a key Steam starts refusing with Settings open (the field that replaces it opens by itself, and stays closed once closed by hand), its notice, a wrongly formatted replacement, one Steam refuses with Enter held down in its field (Steam is asked once) and a good one, and the key never on screen in full; adding from Settings without the language step, and cancelling; the removal dialog naming the account, backed out of and then confirmed); Steam taking a request and never answering it (the app gives up by itself, keeps the game on screen and says Steam could not be reached, and the error goes when Steam answers again); and Steam going off the air with the app open (what is on screen stays, with an error), before it opens (the app comes up with what it had), and before it opens with nothing cached (both tabs say Steam could not be reached, never that nothing was played, and the app recovers when Steam is back).

What the script never opens is the list in step 7.

## Why it exists

This was written down after details shipped that no test could catch: a hover wash wider than its section, a second scrollbar, a line drawn twice, a screen captured that was not the one meant.

## The fake Steam

`main/index.ts` reads `TROPHY_TRACKER_FAKE_STEAM` (and `TROPHY_TRACKER_FAKE_STEAM_HOME`, the home folder holding the fake Steam client) when the app is not packaged; an installed app ignores both, because the user's key is sent to whatever address that is. In that mode the app behaves as on Linux whatever the system: the signed-in account and the stat that feeds each counter come from the fake client folder (`writeFakeSteamFolder`), the running game comes from the fake Web API, and the periodic checks run every two or three seconds instead of every ten and sixty, so the audit does not wait a minute for an unlock, and a request to Steam is given up on after two seconds instead of fifteen (`docs/steam-data.md`), so it does not wait that long for a Steam that never answers. What the fake still does not stand in for: the pictures (art and icons are fetched from Steam's real servers, so without internet the audit only notes that the art did not load), GitHub (the update check always finds nothing) and the Windows registry.
