# Accounts

The rules below are binding; `CLAUDE.md` sends you here before you change anything about accounts: adding, removing, switching, following the Steam client's account, a key and its status, or the account cards.

The app keeps several Steam accounts and follows one at a time. An account is a SteamID, the name and avatar Steam gave for it, and its own Web API key.

The form of the account step is described in `docs/onboarding-form.md`; where the accounts are kept on disk, in `docs/local-data.md`.

## Rules

- **Everything read or written is per account.** `storage/Store.ts` keeps, by SteamID: the key (`config.json`); the library, game views and summaries (`cache.json`); the notes, pins and checklists (`userdata.json`). Achievement lists and art describe the game and are shared.
- **A read belongs to the account it started for**, from its first step to its last: another account may be in use by the time Steam answers. `Tracker` takes the account in use once, as the read starts (`Tracker.owner`), and from then on names it in everything: the key it asks with, what it takes from the store to finish (the library for a game's name and playtime, the summaries the dashboard keeps or reads again: `Store.getLibrary` and `Store.getSummary` take the owner), what it hands to the store, and the in-flight reads it shares. A result for a removed account is discarded.
- **What a read reports afterwards is for that account too.** `KeyStatus.attempt` hands the read two functions bound to the account it started for: `onAnswer`, and `onFailure` for a failure with nobody left to answer (the refresh behind a stale game). A key refused there is marked on the account the game was asked for, never on the one in use by then.
- **A view read for an account that is no longer in use is not sent to the interface.** `Tracker.getGameStaleFirst` calls `onFresh` only while the account it read for is still the one in use; the view is kept in the cache for its own account. The interface files a view under its appid alone, so it would show one account's game to another that owns it too. The decision is `Tracker`'s: `Ipc` only passes the functions on.
- **The interface takes an answer only while its account is still in use** (`sameAccount`, in `docs/store.md`): a game, a dashboard, the current game or the notes asked under one account are dropped when they arrive after another took over, and asked again for that one. An account that was left and followed again is a new stay: what was asked in the first is dropped too, since the second asked again.
- **What the main process pushes by itself follows the same rule.** `Tracker` stops sending the progress of a dashboard (`dashboard-progress`) once its account is no longer in use, though the read goes on and is kept for its account. `GameWatcher` drops what it was observing when the current game is forgotten (`forget({ isCurrentIncluded: true })`): `checkRunningGame` announces nothing and leaves it to the next check, `checkUnlocks` hands no view over, even when the account that took over is on the same game by then, and `refreshCurrent` looks again for the account that took over. A check asked for after the game was forgotten starts at once, beside the one still waiting for the account that was left (`docs/steam-data.md`, one check of each kind at a time).
- **The key never reaches the interface.** `IAccount` carries only its last four characters (`keyEnding`). `ui/components/MaskedKey` is the only way a saved key is shown: no reveal, no copy. A key goes in through `KeyField` and is forgotten by the interface once saved.
- **Key status** (`AccountStatus`): `valid`, `rejected`, `rateLimited`. One `rejected` covers a revoked key and a mistyped one: Steam answers both the same way (403 as HTML).
- `KeyStatus.attempt` records what each read says about the key in use, and tells the interface (`state-changed`) only when the status changes.
- **Only an answer from Steam takes a mark off a key.** A read that works may have asked Steam nothing: a game or a dashboard answered from the cache, or a request that needs no key (an achievement list, art). `Tracker`'s reads call `onAnswer` when a request made with the key worked and the read did, `attempt` hands them the function, and the account is marked `valid` only then, also when it is the refresh behind a stale game that asks. A failure marks the account as before. Every read wrapped in `attempt` passes `onAnswer` on (`Ipc`, the watcher's poll in `index.ts`): one that does not can never take a mark off.
- **A rejected key does not send the user back to the setup.** The app stays open with what it had, `AppShell` shows `KeyTroubleNotice` over the Game and the Dashboard, and "Replace key" in Settings fixes it. The onboarding shows only when there is no account at all.
- **A note, pin or checklist is written for the account it was edited under.** The edit carries that SteamID from the moment it is made (`userData.update`) to `Store.setUserData`, which takes it as the owner, as `setGame` and `setLibrary` do. An edit waits half a second before it is written, and the app may follow another account by itself in that time: a write filed under the account in use on arrival would land in the other account, and an emptied note would delete that account's entry. A write that names an account the app no longer has keeps nothing: it must not bring a removed account's notes back. The undo of a removed checklist item names its account the same way, and does nothing once another account is in use (`userData.restoreChecklistItem`, in `docs/store.md`); the toast that offers it is taken off the screen as its account is left.
- **Switching** (`settings.switchAccount`) flushes pending note edits first: they belong to the account being left. `useAppController` runs `connectStore` again for each account: what was read for the previous one leaves the screen and the other is loaded, instantly when it has a cache.
- **The app follows the account signed in to Steam**, on every system (`services/AccountFollower.ts`). It switches, with a toast, when the client's account changes (asked as the app opens and every 30 s) and **when a game starts**, whatever account was picked by hand. A manual pick therefore stands until a game starts.
- **A read of the client's account that fails is not a sign-out.** `ISteamLocal.getActiveSteamId` answers `null` only when nobody is signed in and rejects when it could not read (`docs/platforms.md`). The follower then keeps what it last saw, so the same account coming back is no change and a manual pick stands. A real sign-out followed by a sign-in on the same account is a change: the app follows that account again.
- **The running game is always the client's account's**, never shown with another account's data. `GameWatcher` asks the follower whose game it is when a game starts. Off Windows, the running game is asked about on the profile of the client's account when the app has it (`RunningGame.ts`), not on the one in use.
- **When the account in use changes, the game on screen is forgotten** (`watcher.forget({ isCurrentIncluded: true })`): it belonged to the account that was left. The rule lives in `services/Accounts.ts`, which every change of account goes through: a switch, a removal, the client's account being followed as the app opens and every 30 s, and an added account taking over. `Ipc` and `index.ts` only call it. After an add the running game is checked again, so a game that is running is asked about anew: it goes to the added account when that is the one playing, and otherwise the app goes back to the account that is. An add that Steam refuses forgets nothing, and the running game is checked again all the same when the app has an account.
- **While a game runs, the account playing it cannot be left** (`GameWatcher.isPlaying`): Settings draws the other cards as not clickable and says why, and the main process refuses the switch.
- If the client's account is one the app does not have, the Game tab says so and offers to add it (`CurrentGame.isOnAnotherAccount`, `AppShell/GameOnAnotherAccount`). That game is neither read nor remembered as the last one played.
- **One card per account** (`AccountCard`, in Settings). Nothing about an account is drawn outside its card. The one in use has the accent border and opens, inside the card, its masked key with "Replace key" and "Remove account" (`Settings/components/AccountDetails`). The field that replaces the key is open while the key is `rejected`, also when it is refused with Settings already on screen, until the user opens or closes it by hand: it is derived from the status, never copied from it into state; any other card is one button that switches to it.
- **A card keeps its shape when the account in use changes:** `AccountCard` is drawn the same way in use or not, and what makes another account clickable is a button laid under the card's content. The same goes for anything that flips between "is a button" and "is not": change what is inside, not the element.
- **Adding.** The dashed card at the end of the list opens the onboarding as the account step alone: no language step, no bar of steps, and no step after it (`navigation.isAddingAccount`), the form already open. The account just added becomes the one in use. A SteamID that is already saved is refused.
- **An add answers a `CheckResult`, as `replaceKey` does** (`SetupService.addAccount`, `Accounts.add`, `IApi.addAccount`): the state on success, the reason on a refusal. The interface checks the pair before it asks (`checkApiKey`, `checkPrivacy`), and the add checks it again as it saves: Steam may answer otherwise this time, and the account may have been saved meanwhile. A refusal is never a state: the form stays open with what was typed and shows the reason, and nothing reaches `settings.acceptAccountStep`.
- **While an account is added to an app that is set up, the store is the only copy of the state.** Each answer of the account step goes into it as it arrives (`settings.acceptAccountStep`), next to what the main process sends by itself (`state-changed`), and leaving the step applies nothing. The first setup keeps a copy only to list its accounts: the store is not wired yet, and taking the first account early would end the setup. It ends on what the main process holds as it ends (`settings.finishSetup` asks for it), never on that copy: the language may have changed since the last account was saved, and the main process may have followed another account, with nobody listening.
- **Removing** an account deletes its key, its cache and its notes, and the dialog says so by name. Removing the last one leads back to the onboarding.
- **In the setup, everything about accounts happens in the account step**, and there is no step after it. An account Steam accepts is saved at once and joins a list of cards above the form, which closes into "Add another account". The step's forward button enters the app.
- The first setup ends on the account signed in to Steam, or else the first added (`settings.finishSetup`).
- An account added in that visit can be removed again in one click. An older one cannot: it may have notes, and Settings asks first.
